# nursery-songs

Short nonsense songs for small children, and the method for making more of
them. No music, no notation, no theory. Just words that are fun to say out
loud in a kitchen.

Written for children aged roughly three to six.

## Making them into recordings

**[suno/ →](suno/README.md)** holds paste-ready Suno exports for all six songs,
already tagged with `[Verse]` / `[Chorus]` / `[Outro]` and matched to a
deadpan / wink / lullaby style prompt. Regenerate any of them with the `suno`
skill.

## Writing one

**[How to write one of these →](HOW-TO-WRITE-ONE.md)**

The short version: find one unpronounceable word and build everything else
around it. Keep the metre boring, rhyme exactly or not at all, never explain
the joke, and let the last verse turn the song around.

## The shape of every song here

- **One hook word** — three syllables, built from ordinary mouth noises
- **One small theme** — a single event, not a feeling
- **Four verses plus a chorus** — never more
- **Call and response** — the leader sings, the room answers
- **A flat joke, repeated** — not clever, and never explained
- **A last verse that turns it** — or refuses to, flatly enough to be a joke
- **Then stop**

## Why this exists

Because "twinkle twinkle little star" is 250 years old and there is nothing in
it for a child to *discover*. Nonsense that rhymes gives a small child
something to do with their mouth and something to predict, which is the
actual pleasure. The words are toys. Hand them over and get out of the way.

## Using them

Sing them badly. Seriously — badly. Do not perform them, do not explain the
nonsense words, and do not add a moral. Every note on how to sing each one is
already in its own file under *How to sing it*.

Use them however you like.
