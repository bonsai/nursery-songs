# Plonk

> **Theme:** one small unhelpful noise, repeated until the kitchen empties out.

## The hook

| Word | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **plonk** | *plonk* | a real sound, not an invented one |

The easiest hook here to pronounce and the hardest to stop saying. Children
will plonk at things for weeks afterwards, which is the actual goal of this
repo.

## Lyrics

**1**

Plonk went the spoon in the cup, one afternoon,
　　plonk, and the soup said, "You're too late."
Plonk went the spoon in the cup, one night,
　　plonk, and the soup said, "I'm fine."

**Chorus**

Plonk, plonk, plonk, in the cup,
　　"nobody told me anything."
Plonk, plonk, plonk — and the spoon fell in.

**2**

Plonk in the teapot at half past two,
　　plonk, and the tea said, "That is new."
Plonk in the teapot at half past three,
　　plonk, and the tea said, "Ask me please."

**Chorus**

**3**

Plonk in the teapot at half past four —
　　the teapot said nothing at all.
Plonk in the teapot at half past five,
　　and the teapot said, "I was going to leave."

**Chorus**

**4 — last**

Plonk, plonk — and the cup left.
　　Plonk, plonk — and the plate left.
Plonk, plonk — and only the spoon stayed,
　　because the spoon had nowhere else to go.

**Chorus — once, quiet, no plonk noises**

## How to sing it

- Actually plonk. Spoon on the table, knuckle on the table, whatever is
  nearby. The song is an instruction, not a description.
- Half past two / three / four / five: say the time out loud. It is a
  countdown and nobody should notice until the end.
- The last chorus has no plonk in it. That absence is the joke.

## Why it works

The teapot escalates from "that is new" to "I was going to leave" without ever
raising its voice. The reversal in verse four is the only event in the song:
everyone leaves, and the culprit is the one thing with nowhere to go.
