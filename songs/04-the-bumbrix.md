# The Bumbrix

> **Theme:** wanting very much to be somebody, and being a round thing.

## The hook

| Word | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **bumbrix** | *bum-briks* | two hard syllables, easy to shout |

Short, percussive, and it breaks slightly on a child's tongue. That's useful
here: the word is nearly unpronounceable and that is why it gets shouted.

## Lyrics

**1**

The bumbrix was a circus,
　　but the bumbrix was a ball.
He rolled onto the trapeeze,
　　and the trapeeze it did fall.

**Chorus**

Bumbrix, bumbrix, round and round,
　　cleverest in town.
Bumbrix, bumbrix, up and down,
　　only a ball. Never a clown.

**2**

He wanted to be a tightrope,
　　he wanted to be a star.
He wanted to be somebody,
　　so he rolled into a car.

**Chorus**

**3**

They gave him a hat and a trumpet,
　　they gave him a cape and a string.
He wore them both very badly,
　　which is the whole of the thing.

**Chorus**

**4 — last**

The circus said, "You're finished."
　　The circus said, "Goodbye."
The bumbrix said, "I'm better."
　　The bumbrix said, "I'm fine."
And he rolled all the way home
　　and nobody saw,
which is how a ball becomes
　　a very small star.

**Chorus — once, gently, slower than before**

## How to sing it

- The chorus is a put-down. Sing it kindly, not meanly. The bumbrix is not
  the villain, he is the audience's favourite.
- "He wore them both very badly / which is the whole of the thing" — the
  second line is spoken almost as an afterthought. Do not build to it.
- Last chorus: drop the volume. Let it feel like a verdict nobody minds.

## Why it works

The chorus states the joke in the plainest possible words and then the song
insists on it for four verses. "You are only a ball. You were never a clown."
has no subtlety at all, on purpose — children do not want subtlety, they want
the thing to be said clearly and then said again.
