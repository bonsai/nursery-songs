# How to write one of these

There is no music here and there is no theory. These are short nonsense songs
for children aged roughly three to six, and every rule below came from
writing five of them badly and then fixing them.

Read [The Gizzmidge](songs/01-the-gizzmidge.md) first. It is the template.

---

## 1. Find the hook before you find the song

The song is not the point. The **word** is the point.

A good hook word is:

- **three syllables**, or two
- **built from ordinary mouth noises** — *plonk*, *snod*, *gizz*, *bum*
- **impossible to say neutrally** — you have to commit to a face
- **wrong in a way that makes an adult hesitate**

Say the word fifty times in a row. If you ever get bored of it, it is not the
hook yet. If you cannot get it out of your head four hours later, it is.

> "gizzmidge" and "wibbledoo" are good. "Smoogle" is not — it is just a
> misspelling of something.

## 2. Pick a small theme, not a feeling

Every song in this repo is about one specific event. Not happiness. Not
friendship. One event.

| Song | The event |
|---|---|
| The Gizzmidge | asks the world for something, gets ignored |
| Snodgrass | refuses to move, forever |
| Plonk | makes one noise until the room empties |
| The Bumbrix | wants to be a person, is a ball |
| Wibbledoo | will not fall asleep |

If you cannot finish that sentence in six words, you do not have a song yet.

**A nonsense song is a very small machine with one moving part.** Every verse
turns the same part one more time.

## 3. Choose: deadpan, or wink

There are two registers and you must pick one before you write a word.

- **Deadpan** (Gizzmidge, Snodgrass) — state the absurd thing flatly, as
  though it were reasonable. Never explain it. This is the harder and better
  register.
- **Wink** (Plonk, Bumbrix) — the song knows it is a joke and the joke is
  stated outright, usually in the chorus.

Do not mix them. A deadpan song that winks in the last verse reads as a joke
with a cold open, and children lose interest at the seam.

## 4. Keep the metre boring

Target **7 or 8 syllables per line**, alternating:

```
There ONCE | was a GIZ | zmid GE |   ← 7, four beats, trochaic
SIT ting | in the MUD            ← 4, the answer, one beat lighter
```

The pattern is always: **statement, then a shorter line that answers it.** That
is a call and response, and it is the oldest device in nursery rhyme. It is
also the reason an adult and a child can sing alternating lines without
falling apart.

Keep the short line at 3–5 syllables. Do not let it creep up to 6.

## 5. Rhyme exactly or not at all

Children hear a near rhyme as a mistake and it makes the song feel broken.

- `all right` / `tonight` / `rain` / `word` — never mix these families
- `cup` / `up` — fine, they rhyme
- `mud` / `good` — fine
- `moon` / `home` — fine

When you are unsure whether two words rhyme, **say both out loud in a row**.
If you hesitate, they don't.

## 6. One flat joke per song, and repeat it

"A hat for a hat" and "a shoe for a shoe" is the joke in The Gizzmidge. It is
not clever. That is the requirement.

A flat repetition is funnier to a four-year-old than a good pun, because they
can predict it, and predicting it is the fun.

Do not explain the joke. Do not add a verse that clarifies. The moment the
song says *and I think that's because…* it is over.

## 7. The chorus must be shoutable in one breath

Test the chorus by trying to sing it twice in a row without breathing.

It has to be:

- **3 or 4 short lines**
- **a name in the first line** (so the room knows what to shout)
- **identical every time**, word for word
- **singable by someone who is not paying attention**

If the chorus needs a breath mid-line, cut words until it doesn't.

## 8. End with a reversal

Look at what the last verse does in each song:

- **Gizzmidge** — the one who ignored him comes back and approves
- **Snodgrass** — nothing changes, and that is the ending
- **Plonk** — everyone leaves and the culprit is left holding the room
- **Bumbrix** — the nobody notices, and that is how he becomes a small star
- **Wibbledoo** — the one thing nobody was asking for, the dove, says *sleep now*

The last verse must **turn the song around** or refuse to turn it around so
flatly that it becomes a joke. Resolve the tension, or don't resolve it at all.
Never do both.

Then **stop.** Do not reprise the chorus three times, do not add a tag, do not
explain. The last line is the last line.

## 9. Never explain the nonsense

This is the rule people break. Nonsense words are not characters with
backstories. You do not tell the child what a gizzmidge is. You do not tell
them where the middlethere is. You do not draw it.

If it needs explaining it wasn't nonsense, it was a description.

## 10. Length limit: 30 lines

Four verses plus a chorus. That's the ceiling and Gizzmidge sits at 28.

A four-year-old's attention at a party is about two minutes, and a call and
response chorus buys you the rest. If your song is 60 lines it does not get
shorter, it just gets skipped.

---

## The four tests

Run all four before you commit.

**1. The read-aloud test.** Read the whole thing out loud, standing up, at
speed. You will run out of breath on the wrong line. Fix that line.

**2. The four-year-old test.** Point at the hook word and ask a small child to
say it three times. If they manage it on the first two tries, the word is too
easy and you need a better hook. If they manage it on the *fourth* try, it is
right.

**3. The embarrassment test.** If at any point while writing it you felt
embarrassed and wanted to make it cleverer — that is the exact moment the song
was working. Go back to that line and make it plainer.

**4. The sing-it-back test.** After a day, without the sheet, try to remember
the chorus. If you can only recall the hook word, the chorus isn't carrying
its own weight. Rewrite the chorus so the hook word alone doesn't do the job.

## House format

Copy this so every song in `songs/` looks the same.

```markdown
# The Name Of The Song

> **Theme:** one sentence, one event, under ten words.

## The hook

| Word | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **the-word** | *how-it-sounds* | why it works in the mouth |

## Lyrics

**1**

First line of the verse,
　　the short answer line.

**Chorus**

Four short lines, shoutable in one breath.

**2** … **Chorus** … **3** … **Chorus** …

**4 — last**

The turn, or the refusal to turn.

**Chorus — [how it ends]**

## How to sing it

- Four bullets. Tempo, dynamics, what to actually do with your body.
- At least one instruction should be a thing to *stop* doing.

## Why it works

One paragraph. If you cannot say why in a paragraph, you do not understand it
yet.
```

Note the two full-width spaces at the start of every answer line — `　　`.
That is an ideographic space, it survives HTML, and it is the only reason the
lyrics line up everywhere. Use it. Do not use a normal space and do not use a
tab.

## What not to write

- A moral, an ending lesson, or anything about being good
- Character names that are real human names
- Anything requiring a vocabulary word a three-year-old lacks
- Performance directions inside the lyrics — they go under *How to sing it*
- Anything that needs the child to already know the tune

The last one matters most. These are songs someone has to learn from the words
alone, standing in a kitchen, badly, with no sheet music in sight. If it
doesn't work read straight off the page, it doesn't work.
